02 · what survivesExecutable criteria don't cause quality. They cause termination.

One control survives every objection, because it holds person, day, scope, genre and model constant:

Immortal Shores — same you, same eight-hour sitting, two halves.

⟶ Mechanics half. Criterion was critical-audit.mjs with process.exit(fail>0?1:0). converged 2h06m

⟶ Visual half. Criterion was 30 rounds of hand-written markdown. 5h33m · never closed

Confluence repeats it across one week: v1 retired a passing executable gate for an unpassable prose one → 261 judge runs in a day → rewrite. v2 replaced prose with scripts that exit non-zero → 8/8 on three consecutive runs.

Prose criteria never terminate. They always find one more complaint, and they rescale between rounds — one judge rescored every dimension 1.5–3 points lower with no regression. A boolean can't do that.

Your games don't fail. They never finish, and then the next one starts. 6 hours between 2342 going dark and CURFEW being created. 110 minutes between NileInSpace's last judge run and the Confluence rewrite.

And the confound that beats gates outright

Every project that stalled on look routes art through a binary pipeline you have to drive by hand — 16 hand-exported .glb; six script versions for one plate. Both projects that came out coherent have zero external assets: Crookemon2's 29 creatures are 218KB of painter code; CURFEW's manifest says "Zero external assets."

When art is code, the agent fixes the defect and re-renders in one turn. When art is a .glb, every round costs a human Blender session and the loop physically cannot close. FI-GRAPHICS.md already mandates this. The failures violated your own doctrine, in the same zip that ships it.

04 · the designFI Floor — three pillars

Named FI Floor, not FI Harness — the store says fi-harness is already the orchestrator cockpit. Mark each pillar below.

P1 Roles, not hex

Every color becomes a semantic role (ink, paper, ground, accent, hazard…). The creator never types a color — they point at one of ~24 hand-tuned palettes rendered as live moving tiles. Must work as canvas fillStyle, SVG fill attribute, and CSS var — CURFEW's art is 43 inline SVG paths and zero canvas.

Why it earns its place: the lever already exists and nobody pulled it — every shell routes color through colors.a/b/c in one file, and E.drawSprite already takes a color argument. It reaches every creator on day one, and it's a removal: the ways to produce an ugly palette drop to zero because the operation isn't offered.

P2 Painters, not files

Art is a function of (role, seed, sim state), shipped as plain JS. Tier 1 stops shipping assets/prompts/AI-PROMPTS.txt and the empty assets/sprites/ — that folder is where art vintages breed. Tiers 2–3 keep a narrow singular-asset lane.

Named risk: FI now owns the art direction of every Arcade game, so the failure mode flips from inconsistent to identical. The structural answer is that a look pack is a parts vocabulary plus a recombination table — CURFEW gets 29 enemies from ~5 bodies and ~20 accessories — but that's an argument, not a proof.

P3 Point the target at something reachable

refs:'required' → 'optional' at all three tiers. Sweep fi-visual-overhaul/SKILL.md:49-51 and the published tier-card copy. Keep rounds 1/3/5, the plateau exit, the honest-FAIL path. Type "like Hollow Knight" and you get a card showing the closest thing your medium can actually reach, rendering live.

Open correction: a mood note must have a destination (palette suggestion, motif seeding), not be formally discarded. Naming games they love is the only design vocabulary a non-expert has.

05 · what got cutThe centerpiece was killed by its own feasibility pass

The synthesis proposed FINISH.html — a seeded bot that plays your game and prints five plain sentences, plus a mutation self-test proving the checks can see. It was the best-sounding idea in the whole exercise. It's cut from v1:

  • It contradicts itself. CURFEW's test.html loads only rng/data-*/engine — never ui.js, art.js, or the 22.5KB stylesheet. It's a headless engine driver, blind to the render layer. A palette check needs the render layer. You can't have both from one runner.
  • It re-derives the APS failure one level up and stamps it with authority.
  • UNKNOWN fires hardest when the creator is most original — and the skill would make repairing FI's introspection harness the agent's top priority.
  • The agent can't own the criterion that ends its own session. Its cheapest repair for "nobody won in eight runs" is always loosen the bot.

What survives, downgraded: ship each shell with a CURFEW-shaped test.html already green, and tell the agent to keep it green — never as a stop rule. It forces a headless-drivable core, which is the real architectural payload of CURFEW.
